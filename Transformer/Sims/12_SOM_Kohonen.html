1. Intuition & Architectural Overview

Imagine throwing a highly flexible, magnetic fishing net over a massive, chaotic pile of differently shaped metal objects.

At first, the net just drapes randomly. But then you turn on the magnets. When a point on the net gets close to a heavy metal object, it snaps to it. Crucially, because it is a net, when one point snaps to an object, it pulls its neighboring strings along with it.

2. Core Architectural Components

  • **The Grid:** The network is usually a 2D grid of nodes (e.g., $10 \times 10$).
  • **The Weights:** Every node on the grid has a weight vector of the exact same dimension as the input data. (If inputting 50-dimensional DNA data, each node has a 50D weight vector).
  • **No Hidden Layers:** The input is connected directly to every node on the grid.
  • $r_c$ and $r_v$: The physical 2D coordinates of the nodes on the grid (e.g., node $c$ is at `[2, 5]` and node $v$ is at `[2, 6]`).

3. Advantages & Trade-Offs

High representational capacity and parameter efficiency, balanced against inductive bias constraints and hardware memory requirements.

Mathematical Formulation & Equations

The following mathematical equations govern the forward pass, loss calculation, and backward pass dynamics visualised in the animation above:

EQUATION 1
$$ c = \arg \min_v \left\| x(t) - W_v(t) \right\| $$
EQUATION 2
$$ \theta(c, v, t) = \exp \left( - \frac{\| r_c - r_v \|^2}{2 \sigma^2(t)} \right) $$
EQUATION 3
$$ W_v(t+1) = W_v(t) + \theta(c, v, t) \cdot \alpha(t) \cdot [x(t) - W_v(t)] $$

        class="code-cmt"># 1. Get the physical 2D location of the BMU
