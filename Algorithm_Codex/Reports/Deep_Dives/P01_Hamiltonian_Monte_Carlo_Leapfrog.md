# P01: Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) & Leapfrog Numerical Integration

## 1. The Beginner's Analogy
Imagine you want to explore the bottom of a deep, winding, foggy valley (a high-dimensional probability distribution).
- **Standard Random Walk (Metropolis-Hastings, E07):** You take blind, tiny, drunken steps in random directions. If you take a step that is too big, you hit a steep cliff and get rejected. If you take a step that is too small, you spend 10,000 years diffusing through the fog like a drop of ink in water.
- **Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC):** You place a frictionless frictionless hockey puck at your current location, give it a random kick (auxiliary momentum $p$), and let it slide along the physical contours of the gravitational potential landscape $U(q)$.
The puck naturally glides smoothly along energy contours, carving out vast, sweeping trajectories that traverse the entire valley in a single leap, maintaining near-perfect energy conservation!

## 2. The Deep Architecture & Mechanics
HMC overcomes the curse of dimensionality in Bayesian posterior inference by mapping probability to physical mechanics:
1. **Phase Space Expansion:**
   Augments target parameter space $q \in \mathbb{R}^D$ (position) with auxiliary momentum vectors $p \sim \mathcal{N}(0, M)$.
2. **Total Energy (The Hamiltonian):**
   $$H(q, p) = U(q) + K(p)$$
   where potential energy $U(q) = -\ln \pi(q)$ is the negative log-posterior, and kinetic energy is $K(p) = \frac{1}{2} p^T M^{-1} p$.
3. **Hamilton's Equations of Motion:**
   $$\frac{dq}{dt} = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p} = M^{-1} p, \qquad \frac{dp}{dt} = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial q} = -\nabla U(q)$$
4. **Symplectic Leapfrog Integration:**
   Standard Euler integration fails because energy spirals outward over time. The **Leapfrog integrator** is symplectic (phase-space volume preserving) and time-reversible, bounding energy errors within narrow oscillations.
5. **Metropolis Acceptance Step:**
   Accepts candidate proposal $(q^*, p^*)$ with probability $\alpha = \min(1, \exp(-\Delta H))$. Because energy is nearly conserved ($\Delta H \approx 0$), acceptance rates typically exceed $80-90\%$ even for long trajectories!

## 3. The Hardcore Mathematics

### Symplectic Geometry & Liouville's Theorem
A differential map $T: (q(t), p(t)) \mapsto (q(t+\tau), p(t+\tau))$ generated by Hamiltonian dynamics preserves the fundamental symplectic 2-form $\omega = \sum_i dq_i \wedge dp_i$.
By Liouville's theorem, phase space volume is strictly conserved:
$$\left| \det \frac{\partial (q(t+\tau), p(t+\tau))}{\partial (q(t), p(t))} \right| = 1$$
Because phase space volume does not expand or compress, the proposal distribution Jacobian is exactly $1$, eliminating the need for complex proposal ratio corrections in the Metropolis step!

### Symplectic Leapfrog Step Update
Given step size $\varepsilon$ and number of leapfrog steps $L$:
1. **Half-step momentum kick:**
   $$p\left(t + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right) = p(t) - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \nabla U(q(t))$$
2. **Full-step position drift:**
   $$q(t + \varepsilon) = q(t) + \varepsilon M^{-1} p\left(t + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)$$
3. **Half-step momentum kick:**
   $$p(t + \varepsilon) = p\left(t + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right) - \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \nabla U(q(t + \varepsilon))$$

### Metropolis Correction
At the conclusion of $L$ leapfrog steps, momentum is negated $p^* \leftarrow -p^*$ to make the proposal time-reversible.
The Metropolis acceptance probability is:
$$\alpha = \min\left(1, \; \frac{\exp(-H(q^*, p^*))}{\exp(-H(q, p))}\right) = \min\left(1, \; \exp\left( - \Delta H \right) \right)$$
where $\Delta H = H(q^*, p^*) - H(q, p) = [U(q^*) + K(p^*)] - [U(q) + K(p)]$.

## 4. The Algorithm Step-by-Step
1. Initialize position $q^{(0)}$.
2. For each MCMC sample $s = 1 \dots S$:
   - Sample random Gaussian momentum $p \sim \mathcal{N}(0, M)$.
   - Evaluate initial total energy $H_0 = U(q) + \frac{1}{2} p^T M^{-1} p$.
   - Simulate $L$ steps of the Leapfrog numerical integrator using gradient $\nabla U(q)$.
   - Evaluate proposed total energy $H^* = U(q^*) + \frac{1}{2} {p^*}^T M^{-1} p^*$.
   - Draw $u \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 1)$. If $u < \exp(H_0 - H^*)$, accept $q^{(s)} = q^*$; else reject $q^{(s)} = q^{(s-1)}$.

## 5. Limitations & Failure Modes
- **Tuning Sensitivity ($L$ and $\varepsilon$):** If $\varepsilon$ is too large, leapfrog diverges and proposals are rejected; if $L$ is too large, trajectories make U-turns and waste computation (leading to the No-U-Turn Sampler, NUTS).
- **Non-Differentiable Topography:** Discontinuous posteriors or step-functions break gradient evaluation $\nabla U(q)$, causing particle reflections.

## 6. Where This Fits
Cross-reference: HMC is the MCMC engine powering probabilistic programming frameworks (like Stan and PyMC). It replaces standard random-walk Metropolis-Hastings (E07) in high dimensions and serves as the continuous physics foundation for Langevin dynamics (P02).
